# Basil's optimal-temperature claims

| Source | Optimal | Stated condition | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| FAO ECOCROP (id 1547) | 18–27 °C | none stated | directly |
| Chang, Alderson & Wright (2005) | 25–30 °C | DLI 20–22 mol·m⁻²·d⁻¹ | via Walters, Tarr & Lopez (2023) |
| Walters & Currey (2019) | 29–35 °C | DLI 19.5 mol·m⁻²·d⁻¹ | via Walters, Tarr & Lopez (2023) |

- **No single window satisfies all three.** ECOCROP and Chang overlap at 25–27 °C; Walters & Currey
  starts at 29 °C, above ECOCROP's maximum.
- **They are not strictly comparable.** The two journal figures were measured at a stated daily light
  integral (DLI, the day's total photosynthetic light). ECOCROP states no condition. A site's air
  temperature alone cannot say which condition applies.
- **Between 27 and 29 °C they point in opposite directions.** Such a site is above ECOCROP's optimum
  and below Walters & Currey's. The CLI prints `Sources disagree:` there, and its conservative read is
  "not optimal (sources disagree in direction)".
- **The conservative read** calls a site optimal only if every source does. For basil none can be.

The two journal figures were read in Walters KJ, Tarr S, Lopez RG (2023), *PLOS ONE* 18(11):e0294905,
doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0294905, © 2023 Walters et al., CC BY 4.0. Figures summarised; changes were
made; not endorsed by the authors.
